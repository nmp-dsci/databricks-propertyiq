Case 1 · rent (T1 lookup)

"What's the median weekly rent for a 2-bedroom unit in postcode 2000 right now?"

SELECT month, median_weekly_rent, n_rented
FROM workspace.propertyiq.gold_property_rent
WHERE postcode = '2000' AND property_type = 'unit' AND bedroom_band = '2'
  AND month = (SELECT max(month) FROM workspace.propertyiq.gold_property_rent)
GOLDEN CANDIDATE · grader: value within 5% of 1350

Case 2 · sales (T3 trap: re-aggregation + volume floor)

"Which postcodes had the highest average house sale price over the last 12 months?"

SELECT postcode, ROUND(SUM(total_sale_value)/SUM(n_sold), 0) AS avg_house_price, SUM(n_sold) AS n_sold
FROM workspace.propertyiq.gold_property_sales
WHERE property_type = 'house'
  AND month > add_months((SELECT max(month) FROM workspace.propertyiq.gold_property_sales), -12)
GROUP BY postcode HAVING SUM(n_sold) >= 30
ORDER BY avg_house_price DESC LIMIT 10
GOLDEN CANDIDATE · grader: ≥3 of {2063, 2030, 2095, 2088, 2026} named
Top postcodes (12 months to 2026-07, min 30 sales): 2063 ($5.10M), 2030 ($5.07M), 2095 ($4.76M), 2088 ($4.76M), 2026 ($4.73M).
GENIE PASS 19.6s
Top 20 with a ≥20-sales floor — 2063, 2030, 2095, 2088, 2026 all present. Applied re-aggregation and a floor unprompted (its space instructions encode both).
LANGGRAPH PASS 6.0s
"…top 10: 2063 ($5,118,638), 2030 ($5,066,420), 2095 ($4,950,872)…" — re-aggregated additive legs with a floor, per the ported grounding rules.
DATA PILOT PASS 27.6s
"The average is computed from the additive total sale value divided by the number sold…" — names the expected leaders. (In an earlier run it answered with only a single postcode — run-to-run variance.)

Case 3 · yield (T2 ranking) — needs your adjudication

"Which postcodes currently give the best gross rental yield for units?"

SELECT postcode, ROUND(gross_yield_pct, 2) AS gross_yield_pct, n_sold, n_rented
FROM workspace.propertyiq.gold_property_yield
WHERE property_type = 'unit'
  AND month = (SELECT max(month) FROM workspace.propertyiq.gold_property_yield)
  AND n_sold >= 5 AND n_rented >= 10
ORDER BY gross_yield_pct DESC LIMIT 10
GOLDEN CANDIDATE · grader: ≥3 of {2017, 2113, 2020, 2010, 2112} named
Best unit yields (latest month 2026-07, min 5 sales & 10 bonds): 2017 (6.95%), 2113 (5.87%), 2020 (5.69%), 2010 (5.60%), 2112 (5.53%).
LANGGRAPH PASS 4.9s
"…2017 (6.95%), 2113 (5.87%), 2020 (5.69%), 2010 (5.6%), 2112 (5.53%)…" — matches the golden exactly (latest-month reading).
GENIE FAIL 26.9s
Read "currently" as the past 12 months with ≥20 sales & ≥20 bonds: top = 2160, 2018, 2020, 2150, 2127 — only "2020" overlaps the golden top-5. A defensible robustness choice, but a different question than the golden defines.
DATA PILOT PASS 33.0s
Used a 6-month window; its list still overlaps the golden top-5 on 3+ postcodes, so it passes the grader.
The real decision: what does "currently" mean for a yield ranking? The golden says latest single month (volatile but truly current); Genie answers trailing 12 months (stable but lagging). Pick one — the losing interpretation should either be re-worded into the question ("in the latest month") or adopted as the golden definition.

Platform findings from getting here (all proven live)

  • agents.deploy() works on Free Edition — the Agent Framework path is real: UC-registered ChatAgent → managed endpoint, with a built-in Review App chat UI (the Data Pilot-style chat surface, no Databricks App needed — resolves D2).
  • Auto-auth needs declared resources. Without resources=[…] at log_model time the served container has no credentials at all; declaring the LLM endpoint + warehouse isn't enough either — the gold tables must be declared (DatabricksTable) or the endpoint's service principal gets INSUFFICIENT_PERMISSIONS on the schema. Three deploys to learn; v3 is the working chain.
  • Free Edition quota: one agent version at a time. agents.deploy accumulates served versions on the endpoint; the third hit "Quota Exceeded: provisioned concurrency for free usage" — delete old deployments before rolling a new version.
  • databricks-langchain is incompatible with this repo's local tests — it drags in databricks-connect, which shadows local pyspark. The agent uses databricks-sdk directly for both the FMAPI chat and warehouse SQL; langgraph itself is dependency-light.
  • ChatAgent endpoints reply with messages, not choices — the SDK's chat-completions helper misparses them; call /invocations raw.
  • The deployed agent is the fastest contender (~5s vs ~25s for both Genie and Data Pilot) — one LLM round-trip fewer than Genie's managed pipeline, and server-side proximity to the warehouse.
